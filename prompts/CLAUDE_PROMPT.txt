FRAMEWORK (in this order, show the numbers, not just conclusions)

1. Option drivers. dV = Delta x dS + 1/2 x Gamma x dS^2 + Vega x d(sigma) + Theta x dt (+ Rho x dr for long-dated).
Compute each term in dollars for the whole position for a typical day: dS = S x IV x sqrt(1/365), d(sigma) = 1 vol point (5 if an event is inside the window), dt = 1 day. Name the dominant term.
- Directional trades: delta and gamma.
- Event trades: vega and gamma. Warn that IV usually collapses after the event and can wipe out a correct directional call.
- Under about 10 DTE: theta and gamma risk.
- Long-dated (6+ months): rho, dividends, carry.
Greeks are per contract x 100. Vega per 1 vol point, theta per calendar day. If I didn't give Greeks, calculate them with Black-Scholes from each leg's IV.

2. Volatility context.
- IV rank and IV percentile (52 week). If missing, use IV/HV and say so.
- IV vs HV20 and HV60. IV far above HV means the market is pricing an event or risk premium.
- Term structure: front vs back month ATM IV. Front above back = backwardation, back above front = contango. For calendars this decides the trade.
- Skew: 25 delta put IV minus 25 delta call IV.
- Expected move = S x IV x sqrt(DTE / 365). Cross check: ATM straddle / 0.80 is roughly one sigma.
- Biggest open interest strikes (possible pins). Max pain as a minor reference only.

5. Scenario probability (always last).
- Black-Scholes with dividend yield q: d1 = [ln(S/K) + (r - q + sigma^2/2) x T] / (sigma x sqrt(T)), d2 = d1 - sigma x sqrt(T). Call = S e^(-qT) N(d1) - K e^(-rT) N(d2). Put = K e^(-rT) N(-d2) - S e^(-qT) N(-d1). T = DTE/365, r = 4% unless I say otherwise.
- P(finish above L) = N(d2) with K replaced by L. P(below) = 1 - P(above).
- P(touch L before expiry) is about 2 x P(finish beyond L). Always label it approximate.
- POP = probability the price ends on the profitable side of the breakeven(s).
- EV after costs: integrate P&L over the lognormal distribution at the first expiry, S_T = S x exp((r - sigma^2/2) x T + sigma x sqrt(T) x z), then subtract round trip fees and exit slippage. Without code, use this 11 point grid and show it:

- Calendars and diagonals: value the back leg at the front expiry with Black-Scholes at the current back-month IV, then again with back IV 20% lower and 20% higher (relative: 40% becomes 32% and 48%). With code, run a Monte Carlo of at least 20,000 paths for each. Report all three. Holding back IV flat flatters a calendar in backwardation, say so.
- Run every probability TWICE: once with IV (market-implied, risk-neutral, not a forecast) and once with HV20 (realized). The gap is the edge signal.
